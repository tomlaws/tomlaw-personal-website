# Nocturne Harbor — Lantern Ward specification

## Art direction

For every future object build, research internet references first and record them in `reference/asset-references.md`. Author the objects in Blender with shaped silhouettes, connected construction and textured PBR surfaces. Plain repeated primitives are only acceptable where they represent actual repeated construction, such as roof tiles or bench slats, and must have appropriate profiles and material detail.

The three references attached on 10 October supersede the glass-tower district. Build a small, inhabited, cartoon miniature with architectural weight: weathered mineral surfaces, recessed windows, selective neon, utility hardware and carefully arranged street furniture. Use fewer buildings so their character is visible. The previous district is preserved in `assets/legacy/before-neighborhood-2026-10-10/`.

## Composition

Three distinct buildings occupy one continuous quay, without a separate square plinth for every building. A broad waterfront street connects narrower access streets and a rear service lane. A delivery passage separates the two taller buildings. The open foreground contains a transit stop and a promenade; a compact two-car cartoon shuttle runs on an elevated railway behind the buildings.

Maintain metre-scale relationships between people, doors, furniture, vehicles and floor heights. Paving, manholes and crossings should explain how the street is used. Do not add separate kerb blocks: recess asphalt 16 cm below the continuous pedestrian slab, soften the exposed pavement edge and provide dropped pedestrian crossings. Avoid evenly repeating lights, trees, parks, facade bands or glowing windows.

Ground paving is flush with the surrounding quay and uses one continuous stone surface. Do not cut drain slots or paving-grid channels into it, and do not add slotted drain grates or raised tile slabs. Grounded street furniture follows the same surface level.

Promenade seats face the harbour, shelter seats face the street, and lamp posts stand outside the swept taxi route. Recess the tree's soil into the paving; use a sculpted branching trunk and broad veined leaves with an asymmetric canopy. Platform height is set by its passenger floor; stairs and a protected station lift connect it to the extended rear quay. The separate cinema lift keeps framed solar glass, a solid machinery back and aligned west-facing landing doors; its floor meets the 0.8 m footway and 4.7 m cinema mezzanine. The building behind the tree is now a completely new picturehouse with textured terracotta, cream mouldings and burgundy enamel, distinct from the repair house's blue-green mineral palette. Its tall sign pylon and low hall replace the previous stacked verandahs and copper vault.

## Materials and lighting

Generate embedded albedo, normal and roughness maps in Blender for mineral-blue render, soot concrete, dusty plaster, clay brick, jade tile, asphalt and cut stone. Texture variation must be visible at normal framing: broad patina, medium surface variation, mortar and fine relief. Project DetailUV in world metres so texture scale is consistent across separate parts.

Encode albedo and emission image pixels as sRGB for both Blender and glTF. Keep normal, roughness and occlusion textures as linear data. Validate representative exported PNG texels against the authored palette to catch a double color-space conversion.

Use softened edges and restrained satin metal. Upper windows are individually recessed and may be unlit. Furnish the shopfront and transit vignette where the objects can be seen. Ceiling-falloff interior textures sit behind separate glass; they are stylized illumination rather than runtime global illumination.

Light every visible surface of open interiors consistently: ceiling, floor, reveals and rear wall. Fixtures belong inside the cabinet or room and should not create an abrupt half-lit facade by pointing away from it. The vending display and vehicle cabins must be hollow, with glazing in front of their contents. Taxi paint, woven canvas, brushed metal, fired roof tiles and indoor/outdoor timber have distinct embedded colour, normal and roughness maps.

Neon sign spill uses a rectangular emitter parallel to, and facing into, its facade. Keep the whole sign case and surrounding halo in front of the emitting plane; never aim these sources diagonally away from the wall. Export the authored width and height so tall blade signs and wide fascia signs retain their different light footprints in the browser.

Night has a dominant cool key, low fill and small warm, pink, cyan and violet accents. Bloom is limited to bright fixtures. Bake occlusion against the whole static neighbourhood with a 3.2 m reach. Reuse a restrained portion of it for direct diffuse contact attenuation because WebGL area lights do not cast shadows. Architectural directional shadows are cached; taxis use inexpensive contact-shadow planes. The sea uses bounded-cadence blurred reflections.

Daytime follows the approved bright isometric reference in `reference/daytime/`: a high-key, low-contrast pass with a pale blue sky and sea, a strong sky fill so shadows stay light (measured island median luminance ≈0.75, shadow floor ≈0.35), and land that reads as warm khaki/olive rather than neutral grey. Paving is warm sand; asphalt sits at about 60% of it, keeps white markings readable and still reads as a recessed road, not a black ribbon. Buildings follow the reference's architecture and tone: one strongly saturated colour per wall, crisp painted cream trim at every floor band, around every window and along the parapet coping, glazing darker than the wall, and foliage a clear green. Street trees keep the promenade and forecourts green. The daytime sea is a saturated azure with real tonal range — deep blue patches against pale sky-lit crests — never a milky grey. Keep daytime architectural reflections faint so they do not muddy the water. Interpolate these colours and intensities continuously into the established cool night palette; night keeps its original exposure, levels and depth, with the asphalt and neon cladding re-tinted so lighter daylight finishes cannot wash the evening out.

Water movement must be visible at the default city framing: overlapping travelling swells, shorter broken ripple highlights and animated reflection distortion. Keep waves in world coordinates, filter fine crests at wide zoom, and animate within the existing single water draw call. Pause and reduced-motion mode freeze the water phase; night retains restrained blue ripple highlights.

## Asset pipeline

1. Run `tools/blender/build_neighborhood.py`, which imports modelling helpers from `build_city.py`.
   `tools/blender/detailed_assets.py` authors the branching tree and ferry geometry; `metro_train.py` authors the cartoon shuttle, `picturehouse.py` the cinema, and `cartoon_station.py` the elevated platform, canopies, access, viaduct and track. The earlier atelier and railway builders in `arcade_quarter.py` are inactive.
   `taxi_sedan.py`, called by `craftsmanship.taxi`, authors the sedan; `preview_taxi.py` retains a separate editable `taxi-source.blend` and renders front/rear close-ups for assembly review.
2. Preserve separate editable objects in `assets/blender/nocturne-source.blend`.
3. Consolidate each asset, then bake and export with `tools/blender/finish_assets.py`.
4. Keep a shared occlusion atlas and shared runtime materials in `nocturne-city.glb`.
5. Load the combined district, four moving asset files and the animated signal lenses; all textures and Draco decoders are local.

The old `build_city.py` entry point remains available for historical reconstruction; current rebuild instructions use `build_neighborhood.py`. Blender and MCP are authoring dependencies only.

`tools/blender/rebuild_neighborhood.py` runs both current pipeline stages in one Blender process. Wait for the compressed export to finish before browser inspection. Vite ignores GLB writes during rebuilding; restart its dev server when introducing a new GLB filename so the public-file index includes it, then reload the preview.

## Performance and validation

Keep the runtime material count within 40 batches. Limit initial resolution to 1.5 million pixels and DPR 1.25, with adaptive resolution. Capture water at 256 px desktop / 192 px mobile, up to 4 Hz and 2 Hz under load. Preserve cached shadows and four-sample edge smoothing.

Runtime optimization (11 October): retain the complete geometry, PBR maps, lights, shadows, five bloom levels and existing resolution budget. Combine bloom's additive blend with the final colour conversion, including Three's source-alpha weighting; avoid a second multisampled scene buffer and unnecessary depth buffers on full-screen effects. Move the existing rectangular-light back-plane rejection before the LTC texture lookups, without new distance cutoffs or removing lights.

Prepare the linear HDR shader variants with `compileAsync` and upload textures in short batches behind the loading screen, including offscreen vehicles. Reveal the scene after its first rendered frames. Use demand rendering: animations and live time request continuous frames, controls wake the view, atmosphere transitions finish while paused, and the final water reflection refreshes after camera movement. Once a paused preset settles, stop drawing; wake on visibility changes. Preserve reduced-motion behaviour.

Development profiling is opt-in with `?profile=label`: 120 warm-up frames followed by 360 visible frames, asynchronous GPU timing, CPU submission timing, draw counts and shadow/reflection buckets. `&pipeline=legacy` provides the original lighting and postprocessing path for comparison; `&still` freezes the initial pose for image comparison. These switches and the audit are excluded from production. Keep results tied to viewport, pixel ratio and actual GPU; desktop results do not establish mobile performance. Renderer regression checks run with `npm test`.

Implementation references: [Three WebGLRenderer compilation and texture initialization](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/WebGLRenderer.html), [React Three Fiber demand rendering](https://r3f.docs.pmnd.rs/advanced/scaling-performance), and the installed Three r180 `UnrealBloomPass`, `OutputPass`, `WebGLState` and `lights_physical_pars_fragment` sources. The asset files remain unchanged for this runtime-only work; no Blender rebuild is required.

Validate the current manifest's assets, embedded maps, glazing, room UVs, shared occlusion and metre-scale vehicles. Test lighting continuity, traffic closure, train and platform clearance, lift bounds, ferry wrapping and isometric projection. Inspect the actual browser in day/night, from another angle and at mobile width; check runtime errors and the production build. Report measured performance with its test environment.

The Blender build must also pass geometric checks before export: no Chinese lettering; both roof pitches face upward; actual sightlines through each shopfront reach its furnishings; the delivery lane meets the main road without a blocking curb; the lift cabin aligns with both floors and clears the shaft roof; paving follows the seawall footprint and pier supports reach below the waterline.
